# Design contract

Progression looks and behaves like one product because every page follows the same small set of rules. This file is the contract; `npm run lint:ui` (apps/web/scripts/ui-lint.mjs) enforces the parts a script can check, and the route crawl in the verification notes checks the rest in a browser. Read this before adding a page or a panel.

## Look

Vercel and shadcn inspired: neutral zinc surfaces, 1px borders, Geist type, black primary actions, one blue accent for links, focus and selection. Colour is reserved for status, data and two decorative touches: the tinted icon chip on a KPI tile and the soft tint of an application icon in the navigator. No gradients. Tokens live in `apps/web/src/styles.css`; pages never use arbitrary hex colours, and status colours come from `apps/web/src/lib/statusColors.ts`.

**Brand.** The wordmark (`apps/web/public/logo.png`) sits top-left of the expanded navigator, in the phone top bar, on the login page and on the public status page. When the navigator is minimised to its 60 px rail only the mark (`mark.svg`, the red swoosh) remains, 28 px, centred, with the same tooltip the collapsed application icons use; the same vector is the favicon and the source of the touch and home-screen icons. The logo colours never become tokens: nothing in the UI paints in the logo red or navy, and the fade inside the mark is artwork, not a UI gradient.

The portal home keeps the hero alone. Nothing else uses the hero. |

## Navigator

The staff navigator has six sections in a fixed order, each a collapsible heading: Insight (Dashboards, Reports), Service desk (Tickets, Knowledge), Service operations (Operations, Changes, Field service, Teams), Accounts (Customers, Contracts & scope, Service catalog), Infrastructure (Configuration (CMDB), Assets) and System (Administration). The portal has Overview above three sections: Support (Service status, My tickets, Approvals, Knowledge), Your services (Services & contracts, Assets, Maintenance & visits) and Account (Reports, Users). The trees live in `apps/web/src/layouts/nav.ts`; an application's label is the label of its `APPLICATIONS` entry in the application map, and the applications appear in that map's order.

An application lists its modules only when two or more are visible to the person (the same rule `ModuleNav` applies to a strip), and the module strips in `apps/web/src/layouts/modules.ts` mirror the navigator's children entry for entry with the same permissions; `apps/api/test/navigation.test.ts` enforces both, together with the labels, the order, the role visibility and the administration rail. Sections collapse from their heading and the choice is remembered per browser (`localStorage`), while the application you are on stays visible inside a collapsed section; the *Filter navigator* box ignores headings and collapse state. The 60 px rail shows one icon per application with a tooltip and a divider between sections; the phone bar shows the first five visible applications with their `short` labels. The Dashboards children are pages of their own (Overview at `/`, then `/dashboards/noc`, `/dashboards/soc`, `/dashboards/amc` and `/dashboards/my-work`, each behind its dashboard permission; a custom dashboard appends after My work), so the row that is highlighted is the page you are on whatever filters the URL carries. Ticket quick views are modules with a query string, which are highlighted only on an exact match of path and query (adding a filter keeps the application row active and drops the module highlight).

## Interactive and local filters

A dashboard has exactly two kinds of filter, and they look different so nobody confuses them.

**Interactive filters** sit in the hero's filter row and apply to every panel on the page: Period (7, 30 or 90 days), Scope (one customer or all) and, on Network operations, Team. Changing one re-queries the whole page; the scope line under the strip ("Showing All customers · last 30 days (8 Sep – 7 Oct) · All NOC teams") says in words what is applied, and the Dashboards strip carries the period and the scope along so moving between dashboards never loses them. They live in the URL (`days`, `customerId`, `teamId`) through `useListState`, so a filtered dashboard can be bookmarked or shared and a Grady link opens it filtered. On a list the equivalent is the filter bar.

**Local controls** sit in one panel's header (`Panel` `action` slot) as a small `Segmented` or one compact `Select`: one to three per panel, never a period, always a client-side view of data the page already holds. A local control changes its own panel and nothing else: no tile, no other panel, no re-query. Its state lives in the URL under the panel's own key (`useLocalControl` in `components/dashboards/localControls.ts`: `flow`, `age`, `heat`, `by` on the Overview; `crit`, `risk`, `eng`, `by`, `events`, `heat` on the NOC; `flow`, `cust`, `events`, `heat` on the SOC; `queue`, `work`, `by` on AMC; `resolved`, `team` on My work; the default value stays out of the URL) so the back button restores it. Changing either kind never changes the pathname, never remounts the page's content and never scrolls it.

Nothing else on a dashboard filters the dashboard. A tile, a slice, a bar, a cell or a row is a door into the list (next section), never a filter on the page it sits on.

## Filtering and drill-down conventions

These are the conventions the dashboards and lists follow. They match how ServiceNow (Platform Analytics, Service Operations Workspace) and the current MSP platforms behave, so people arriving from those tools find nothing surprising.

| Convention | How ServiceNow does it | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| A number is a door | Every KPI ("single score"), chart segment and list count drills into the record list with the same encoded query; it never filters the dashboard it sits on | Every tile, donut slice, bar and breakdown row links to `/tickets?…` through one shared link builder carrying the exact predicates; dashboards have no filter-in-place |
| Dashboard-wide filters are explicit | "Interactive filters" sit at the top (globally or per tab), apply to every widget, and are visibly separate from drill-down | Period and customer scope (and team on NOC) live in the hero; a scope line and filter chips show what is applied; changing them re-queries every widget |
| Widget-local controls only change that widget | Per-visualisation grouping and series choices | One to three controls in the panel header, state in the URL, client-side over data the page already holds; tiles and other panels never move |
| Lists show their filter as a breadcrumb | "All > Active = true > Priority = 1", each condition removable, "All" clears, saved filters | Applied chips are a breadcrumb row ("All open > Incidents > Unassigned"), each removable, the root resets to the page default, counts read "1–25 of 148", saved views are offered from the bar |
| Quick filters do not reprice themselves | Landing-page KPIs are fixed counts; filtering happens in the list | List tile counts are computed over the base scope without the tile dimensions, so they stay put when one is active; nothing scrolls |
| The SLA engine owns "breached" | Reports use `task_sla.has_breached`, set by the engine job | "Breached" is an SLA row the engine flagged (`state = 'breached'`; the sweep runs every minute, so the lag is at most a minute); "at risk" is a running clock the engine has warned on; the list, every dashboard, the reports and the assistant all use the one predicate (`apps/api/src/modules/sla/predicates.ts`) |
| Role landing page | SOW landing: greeting and shift, "My work" assignment donuts, announcements, upcoming tasks | Overview: greeting with the person's current shift, "My day" numbers, assignment donut, announcements, upcoming (visits, maintenance, CAB, change windows), then desk health and the operations strips the person may see |
| Dashboard tabs | A dashboard picker and tabs at the top | A module strip on every dashboard page (Overview · NOC · SOC · AMC · My work · custom later), plus the navigator children |
| Configuration tables filter like record lists | Every table in the platform, system tables included, opens with the same list filter, breadcrumb and paging | One toolbar on every configuration page: a search over the page's columns, filter pills, *Include inactive* where rows can be switched off, the breadcrumb with a "12 of 48" count and paging at 50 rows, with the state in the URL |

Every row of the table is shipped: the drill-down links and the guard test, the hero's interactive filters with the scope line, the local controls in the URL, the breadcrumb and the quiet quick filters on lists, the one SLA vocabulary, the Overview landing page, the Dashboards strip with the navigator children, and the administration toolbar on every configuration page.

## Stat tiles and breakdown rows

A click always shows the matching records, and nothing ever scrolls the page.

- **On a dashboard or an overview page the tile is a door.** It is a router link (`to` on `KpiItem`) built with `ticketListPath` from `@itsm/shared`, carrying every predicate the number was counted with: the customer scope, the domain, the type, the SLA state, and the period as a `createdFrom/To` or `resolvedFrom/To` range when the number is period-bound. The list it opens shows the same number in its count line, and `apps/api/test/dashboards-consistency.test.ts` holds every dashboard to it. Panel "View all" links and breakdown rows (`href` on `BreakdownItem`, `RowList` rows, `WorkloadList` rows) follow the same rule. A tile with nothing behind it has no hover; the arrow appears on hover and the hint names the destination when it leaves the module ("View in Tickets"). Dashboards never filter themselves in place: a tile toggle that used to narrow a panel on the same page is now a link. A row whose subject is a record (a customer in the Overview's *Needs attention* panel, a contract, a visit) opens that record, and the ticket counts beside it are their own doors into the list; only a figure that is a count of tickets is a door.
- **On a list page the tile is a quick filter.** One tile is the baseline (the *Open* tile on Tickets) and never filters; the others toggle one condition in place (`onClick` and `active` on `KpiItem`), show an active ring, and add a chip to the breadcrumb. The tile figures are counted over the scope above them (search, customer, priority, team, service, the type tab) without the status chips or the tile toggles themselves (`GET /tickets/stats` strips `statusCategory`, `statusId`, `open`, `slaState`, `breachRisk`, `knownError`, `mine`, `unassigned`, `watching` and `assigneeId` from the tile aggregates), so clicking one never changes another and the *All* type pill is the sum of the type pills before any click. Breakdown rows (`onSelect` on `BreakdownBar`) filter the list in place the same way. The CSAT page's tiles ignore the rating, channel and low-ratings filters for the same reason.
- `window.location.href` is never used, and `scrollIntoView` has no place in a filter: the person stays where they clicked.

## Dashboards

Every staff member lands on the **Overview** (`/`): a greeting with the shift running now (from the handover rotas), *My day* (assigned, breached, at risk, resolved today, approvals, due today, each a door into their own list, and their queue by type as a donut), *Announcements* for staff, *Upcoming* (visits, maintenance, CAB meetings and change windows today and tomorrow, each opening its record), the **Operations** strips (one card per dashboard the person may open, showing that dashboard's own tile numbers with an *Open dashboard* link that keeps the period and the scope), the **Service desk** block for holders of `tickets:read` (five KPIs: Open, Resolved and SLA compliance with the change against the previous period and a sparkline, Breached with the breaches delta and sparkline, and Unassigned as a live count alone, because assignment keeps no history to compare with; then Ticket flow, Open by type, When tickets arrive, Backlog ageing, Who carries the load, Changes and Response by priority) and the **Management** block for holders of `dashboards:management` (account counts, Needs attention, Customer satisfaction, Expiring contracts, Entitlements near limit). The sections follow the person's permissions and the API answers one payload (`GET /dashboards/overview`), so the page never shows a number the person could not open. The dedicated dashboards are pages of their own with the same frame: **Network operations**, **Security operations**, **AMC & field service** and **My work**; the dashboard strip and the navigator children list them, and an old `/?view=` link redirects to the page.

The vocabulary every dashboard shares, and what each word links to:

| Word | Meaning | The door it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Open | status category new, open or pending | `/tickets` (the page default) with the dashboard's scope and domain |
| Breached | an SLA clock the engine flagged (`state = 'breached'`) | `slaState=breached` |
| At risk | a running clock the engine has warned on, nothing breached yet | `slaState=at_risk` |
| Within SLA | not breached (on track or at risk) | `slaState=ok,at_risk` |
| Likely to breach | the breach-risk job scored the ticket high | `breachRisk=high` |
| Unassigned | no assignee | `assignee=unassigned` |
| Opened · Nd, Resolved · Nd | created or resolved inside the period (whole days in UTC, as the list cuts a date-only bound; only the arrivals heatmap uses the scope's timezone) | `statusCategory=any` plus `createdFrom/To` or `resolvedFrom/To` |
| Opened today, Resolved today | the same for the current day | the same with today as the range |
| A bar of a daily flow | that day's opened (or resolved) tickets | the day as the created (or resolved) range |
| An ageing bar | the tickets created inside the bucket's exact window | the window as `createdFrom/To` with the segment's SLA state |
| A heatmap cell (last 7 days) | the tickets created in that hour, in the scope's timezone | the hour as `createdFrom/To`; the whole-period pattern has no door |
| A donut slice, a breakdown row | the record the slice or row stands for (type, priority, severity, team, engineer, service, customer, category, site) | the record's id in the matching key |
| A strip number | the dedicated dashboard's own tile (one function computes both) | the same link the tile carries |

A number that cannot be a door stays a figure: "Due today" (no list predicate), the SLA-compliance percentage, the response and resolution times, the change outcomes (implemented, failed, backed out are not list filters), monitoring and SIEM event counts (events, not tickets; the panel links to the event feed), the whole-period arrival pattern. Everything else links through `ticketListPath` from `@itsm/shared`, and `apps/api/test/dashboards-consistency.test.ts` holds every door of every dashboard to the list's own count.

Charts follow one set of rules: one value axis per chart (never a dual axis); categorical hues from `packages/shared/src/palette.ts` assigned in a fixed order and never cycled, at most six slices then *Other*; a legend whenever a chart holds two or more series; a single-hue sequential ramp for magnitude (the heatmap); the status colours (`STATUS_COLORS`) only for a series that means a state (breached, resolved, completed) and never as an identity colour; numbers and labels in the text tokens, never in the series colour; a donut falls back to bars when its largest slices are too close to compare by eye. The kit is `TrendChart` (bars with `pointHref` for drill-down, areas, lines), `DonutChart`, `HeatmapGrid`, `BreakdownBar`, `SlaGauge`, `KpiGrid` and the shared panels in `components/dashboards/DeskPanels.tsx` (`AgeingPanel`, `ArrivalsPanel`, `EventsPanel`, `RateTable`).

**Extension point.** The dashboards are listed in `components/dashboards/registry.ts` (`DASHBOARDS`: key, route, label, permission, lazy page component). A new dashboard, built-in or custom, is one entry there, one route in `routes.tsx`, one page in `packages/shared/src/appmap.ts`, one child under Dashboards in `layouts/nav.ts` and one entry in `DASHBOARD_MODULES` (`layouts/modules.ts`); `apps/api/test/navigation.test.ts` and `apps/api/test/ai-appmap.test.ts` hold the five in step, and the page renders inside `DashboardFrame` so it filters like the others. Custom dashboards that a person composes are not built yet; when they are, a `user_dashboards` model (owner, name, sharing with roles or teams, a layout of widgets where each widget names a panel from the kit, its data source among the dashboard payloads and its local control) would feed the registry at runtime with one entry per saved dashboard under `/dashboards/<slug>`, and nothing else in the frame, the strip or the navigator changes.

## Lists

The conditions a list is showing read as a breadcrumb under the filter bar (`ListShell`): a root chip for the page's default view (`breadcrumbRoot`, "All open" on Tickets, "All" elsewhere), then one removable chip per condition in the order it was applied, which is the order of the keys in the URL. A dashboard link therefore reads back as its predicates ("All open > NOC, General, AMC, Service desk > Incidents > Unassigned"), a period-bound link shows its date range as a chip ("Created: 7 Sep – 6 Oct"), and a comma list of ids reads as its labels. Clicking the root chip, or the bar's reset action (`clearLabel`), returns to the default view; removing one chip clears only its key(s). The count line beside the breadcrumb reads "1–25 of 148 open incidents": the page range, the total, and the noun the breadcrumb implies (the status word when the view is the default or one category, the type when a tab is on). Status chips offer the categories and *Any status*, which lifts the status filter altogether (`statusCategory=any`; the page omits the key when it calls the API). Saved views (`/saved-views`, entity `ticket`) appear as the first pills of the quick row and a *Save this view* pill sits at the end of the bar once a condition is applied. Older links that still carry `open=true`, `mine=true`, `unassigned=true`, `assigneeId=` or `status=` are rewritten into the page's own keys on arrival, so one breadcrumb describes every list.

## Administration toolbar

Every configuration page under Administration, and the Knowledge categories page, carries the same toolbar above its `ConfigTable`: `components/admin/ConfigToolbar.tsx`, driven by `hooks/useConfigFilter.ts`. The bar is the lists' own `ListFilterBar` surface: a debounced search box, one `FilterGroup`/`FilterOptions` pill per select showing the count each option would leave under the other conditions, the *Include inactive* switch only where rows carry an active flag, the Reset action, and a slot at the end of the bar for the page's own controls (the list picker leads the bar on Option lists through `lead`, Refresh sits in the slot on the outbox, a date-range pill goes in `extra` on the audit log). Under it sits the same `BreadcrumbRow` the lists use: the root chip *All* (the page's default view, which resets the filter keys and nothing else), one removable chip per condition in the order the keys appear in the URL ("All > Search: network > Domain: NOC > Including inactive") and the count line, which reads "48 services" when nothing narrows the view and "12 of 48 services" otherwise; the total is the rows of the view, so active rows unless *Include inactive* is on, and the noun is the page's own.

Filtering is client-side over the rows the page already holds (`lib/configFilter.ts`, a pure module pinned by `apps/api/test/config-filter.test.ts`): every space-separated term of the search must match one of the page's named columns (a team's members, a service's line and SLA, a rule's recipients count as columns), a select is a single-choice predicate, inactive rows stay out of the view until `inactive=1`, and the rows page at 50 through `ConfigTable`'s `pager`. The state lives in the URL through `useListState` (`q`, the select keys, `inactive`, `page`; a default stays out of the URL and a changed condition returns to page 1), so a filtered view can be bookmarked, carried by a link from the assistant and restored by the back button; every page declares its keys as `filters` in `packages/shared/src/appmap.ts`. Users and the Audit log keep their server-side filtering behind the same bar in controlled mode (`serverToolbar`): their own `useListState` keys feed the search box, the pills, the breadcrumb and the count ("12 of 48 users", with the unfiltered total from a one-row query; "1,204 entries" on the audit log, whose Date pill writes `from` and `to` as one chip). A reorder action (Move up and Move down on option lists, custom fields, CI types, assignment and escalation rules and risk questions) moves the row past the row shown next to it (`moveBeside` in `lib/configFilter.ts`, `moveNextTo` in `components/admin/api.ts`), so an inactive row hidden from the default view keeps its place and never swallows a move; the arrows are disabled while a search or a pill narrows the view (`useConfigFilter().narrowed`; *Include inactive* alone keeps them), and at the first and last row shown. Empty states read "No services match" with the hint to clear the breadcrumb when a condition is applied and keep their own wording otherwise. A page with no active flag offers a Status pill instead when the rows have a lifecycle (API keys: Active, Expired, Revoked), and a window with an end date counts as inactive once it has passed (change blackouts).

`npm run lint:ui` holds the rule `config-table-without-toolbar`: a page that renders `<ConfigTable` without `<ConfigToolbar` fails, and a true sub-panel table opts out with a `// ui-lint: no-toolbar` comment. The toolbar's test ids are `config-toolbar`, `filter-bar`, `config-search` (the input), `config-inactive` (the switch's wrapper), `filters-reset`, `applied-filters`, `breadcrumb`, `breadcrumb-root`, `breadcrumb-chip[data-key]` and `list-count`.

## Buttons, dialogs, tables, states

- Primary is black, at most one per header; `outline` for secondary actions; `ghost` for icon and minor actions; `danger` for destructive ones. `sm` inside cards and toolbars, `md` in page headers and dialogs.
- Records open on their record page. Short forms and confirmations use `Dialog` / `ConfirmDialog`; previews and long forms use `Drawer`. Destructive actions always confirm.
- `DataTable` with `dense` on lists, clickable rows open the record, identifiers in mono, the tinted header, pagination in the card footer; the shells leave room under the last card so a pager scrolls clear of the assistant launcher.
- Loading uses `LoadingBlock` / `KpiSkeleton`, empty uses `EmptyState` with an icon and one next step, errors use `ErrorBlock` with retry.
- Enter animations run once when a page mounts, never when a filter changes.
- Boards use the browser's native drag and drop; no drag library. A lane the card may not land in is greyed while it is dragged, the WIP figure in a lane header turns amber at the limit and red over it, and nothing is ever blocked by an indicator.
- Customer-facing wording is edited in a `Dialog` with a Write-with-Grady helper and a notify checkbox; the record never shows internal fields to customer users.
- Ratings use `RatingStars` (five buttons in a radio group, 44 px targets on phones and the public page, colours from `CSAT_RATING_COLORS`) and `RatingBadge` in lists; the survey prompt is one `SurveyCard` shared by the portal ticket and the public page. The public survey and status pages render `PageHeader` under their own wordmark header, outside both shells.
- The assistant panel (`components/grady/`) never opens a dialog: a proposal is a card inside the thread (preview, change lines, record count, tier, expiry, Confirm primary and Cancel outline, bound to the server's action id), progress is one quiet line per tool, feedback is two thumbs under the reply with a one-line note in place, and navigation the assistant performs is announced with a toast. The window stays mounted while minimised.
- Preference matrices (`components/notifications/PreferenceMatrix`): rows are categories, columns are channels; a cell is a checkbox saved on click with an optimistic tick that rolls back with the server's message; a locked cell is a lock icon with the tooltip "Always on (set by your administrator)"; a column that cannot be used is disabled as a whole with its reason on one line above the rows; an always-on column is a muted tick; the rows stack under `sm` with a small label beside each cell. A mobile number is verified in a `Dialog` that shows the masked number and takes a one-time code, with "Send again" after a minute.
- One-time codes and masked numbers: a code the person sends from their phone is shown once in large monospace with a live countdown and an "Open WhatsApp" link; a code sent to the phone is typed into a six-digit numeric input. A mobile number is shown masked as `+91••••1003` on the profile card, the Users pages and the inbound log, with the masking done by the API, never in the browser. The link state is one badge (`WHATSAPP_LINK_COLORS`: Linked, Verified · chat off, Waiting for your code, Not verified) with one primary action beside it, and the inbound log is a `ConfigTable` whose outcome badges come from `WHATSAPP_INBOUND_COLORS`.

## Printed documents

The report document (HTML and PDF, `apps/api/src/modules/reports/theme/`) is a design system of its own and not the application UI: a light sheet, the brand navy for headings, the cover title and section rules and the brand red for the cover stripe and section markers (never for text or data), both overridable from settings, the Geist typeface and the Progression wordmark embedded. The two brand colours appear only on documents. Charts use the validated palette in `theme/tokens.ts`: categorical slots in a fixed order and never cycled (a fifth series folds into Other in the de-emphasis gray), one sequential ramp for heatmaps, the status scale only for series that mean met, breached, completed or missed, and the emphasis rule (one category in the first hue, the rest gray) when a chart makes one point. Marks are at most 24 px thick with a rounded data end, lines are 2 px with an end dot, gridlines are hairlines, direct labels are used where they fit, a legend appears for two or more series, a donut shows at most six slices and the centre total and falls back to bars when the largest slices are close, and every chart has a table twin in the sections or the Excel workbook. The executive summary follows one anatomy: the masthead, a one-line summary, six KPI tiles (value, delta badge against the previous period coloured by whether the move is good, target, sparkline), the remaining tiles compact, then the three lists What went well, What needs attention and Next steps, then two headline charts. Sections print under chapter headings with an intro line, charts two per row (a heatmap full width), callouts with a green, amber or blue rail and zebra tables with a totals row. On screen the same result is shown by `ReportPreview` with the dashboard kit (tiles with the delta hint, the `InsightsCard` with the three lists, `TrendChart`, `BreakdownBar` for donuts, `SlaGauge` for gauges and a compact grid for heatmaps) and chapters as labelled groups of cards.

## Checking it

`npm run verify` runs the typecheck and `lint:ui`. The lint fails on a page reload through `window.location`, an arbitrary hex colour, the retired `secondary` variant, an `<h1>` outside the shared headers, a page without a shared header, and a `ListShell` page without an empty state. The browser crawl (`r13-audit.cjs` in the session scratchpad, kept with the verification notes) opens every route as an administrator, a NOC engineer and a portal user at 1440 and 420 pixels wide and asserts the header kind, the filter bar on list pages, no horizontal overflow and no console errors.
